Sencha

Japan’s everyday green tea: grassy, bright and quietly sweet, made from leaves that are steamed, rolled and dried.

Types of Sencha

Sencha is grown in full sun. What sets one cup apart from another is mostly how long the leaves are steamed and when they are picked. Choose a style below.

Three small facts

  • Born in 1738Nagatani Soen of Uji is credited with perfecting the method of steaming, rolling and drying leaves that gave us modern sencha.
  • Cooler water, sweeter cupBrewing around 70–80°C keeps bitterness in check. Boiling water pulls out astringency and flattens the flavor.
  • The eighty-eighth nightShincha is picked in spring, and tea picked around the eighty-eighth day after the start of spring is said to bring a long, healthy life.

Well-known Japanese houses

Ippodo Tea
Kyoto tea merchant dating to 1717, admired for refined, balanced leaf tea.
Ito En
Tokyo company behind the Oi Ocha line, among Japan’s best-known bottled and leaf teas.
Fukujuen
Kyoto house founded in 1790, with a wide range of sencha and gyokuro.
Yamamotoyama
Tokyo tea maker with roots in the late 1600s, popular with tea drinkers abroad.
Nakamura Tokichi
Uji tea shop established in 1854, known for matcha and fine sencha.
